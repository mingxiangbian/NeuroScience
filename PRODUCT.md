# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The primary user is the workspace owner, returning over months or years to study, review, and extend a personal NeuroScience x AI knowledge base. Secondary viewers may include mentors, collaborators, or readers who need to understand the structure of the research, but the default design should optimize for repeated personal use rather than public marketing.

The most important active surfaces are the long-term project readers under `projects/`, especially the Foundations roadmap and Language / IELTS reader. The root homepage and the `papers/brain-memory-for-ai-agents` memory project are already close to the desired level and should not be treated as the main redesign targets unless explicitly requested.

## Product Purpose

This workspace turns scattered study material, research questions, paper notes, source maps, and project plans into a durable, navigable knowledge system. Success means the user can quickly re-enter a topic, inspect the current state of a project, follow links between evidence and conclusions, and keep using the pages without visual fatigue.

## Brand Personality

Careful, archival, and visually crafted.

The product should feel like a serious personal research instrument with a distinct visual point of view. Aesthetic quality is a first-order requirement: the interface should be pleasant enough to invite long use, but still support scanning, comparison, and focused reading.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic AI-generated UI: template SaaS panels, repetitive card grids, decorative gradients, stock dashboard tropes, excessive glass effects, and design moves that look impressive in isolation but make the knowledge base harder to use.

Do not flatten the workspace into a plain documentation site. Preserve the existing Cyber Ink / rice-paper / deep-ink identity where it is already established, but avoid spreading ornamental effects into dense reader surfaces when they reduce clarity.

Do not spend redesign energy on the root homepage or the `papers/brain-memory-for-ai-agents` memory project unless the user asks for those surfaces directly.

## Design Principles

Prioritize long-use beauty: make pages visually satisfying without sacrificing repeated reading and navigation.

Preserve the research-workspace identity: rice-paper surfaces, deep ink, restrained cinnabar or blue accents, and the twin-arc mark can anchor continuity when they support the task.

Design for re-entry: every project page should make the current module, status, next action, and surrounding context easy to recover after time away.

Keep the system personal but durable: optimize for the owner's real workflows first, while keeping the structure understandable to a future collaborator or reader.

Target the right surfaces: use future Impeccable critique, audit, polish, and live sessions primarily on `projects/foundations` and `projects/language`.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

There are no project-specific accessibility requirements beyond keeping the pages readable and operable in normal browser use. Visual quality takes priority, but future design work should still avoid obvious readability failures, text overflow, or motion that blocks use.
