# How to build each knowledge card

For every Part 2 topic, collect:

1. **Story title**
2. **Core change, event, person, place, object, or experience**
3. **Background**
4. **Cause or motivation**
5. **Specific actions**
6. **One memorable scene**
7. **Results**
8. **Feelings and reflection**
9. **Useful vocabulary and phrases**
10. **Other cue cards that can reuse the story**
11. **One-minute preparation keywords**

Do not write a full script first. Build the raw material, organise it, and then practise speaking from keywords.

# Core Story 001 — From Waiting to Taking Initiative

## Why this is a core story

This is **one personal experience that can answer several different IELTS Part 2 cue cards**. Do not memorize two separate answers.

The central story is:

> I used to be more passive and wait for opportunities or help to come to me. Around the end of my third year of college, I started to believe that I should take more initiative. One concrete change was the way I studied: instead of relying mainly on memorization, classes, and teachers, I began using AI actively as a personal study assistant, especially for IELTS speaking practice.

The facts stay mostly the same. **Only the spotlight changes according to the cue card.**

---

## Cue card A — Describe a change that you made recently

You should say:

- what the change was
- what caused the change
- what you did for the change
- how you feel about the change

### Best spotlight for this cue card

Focus mainly on the **study-routine change**.

- **Before:** I studied more passively, especially through vocabulary memorization, lectures, online courses, and material prepared by other people.
- **Change:** I began using AI actively as a learning assistant and added much more speaking practice.
- **Cause:** I needed a more efficient and personalized way to prepare for IELTS, and I had started to believe that I should take more initiative instead of waiting for the perfect learning environment.
- **Action:** I began having live English conversations, asking for explanations and corrections, building phrase and Part 2 story banks, and using AI to clarify difficult academic ideas.
- **Result:** My learning became more active, personalized, and efficient.

### Possible opening

> One important change I made recently was changing the way I study. I used to rely much more on passive learning, but now I try to take more initiative and use AI actively as a study partner.

---

## Cue card B — Describe a time when you changed an important opinion of yours

You should say:

- when you changed your opinion
- what your original opinion was
- why you changed it
- how you felt about the experience

### Best spotlight for this cue card

Focus mainly on the **mindset change**.

- **When:** Around the end of my third year of college.
- **Old opinion:** I believed that opportunities depended heavily on luck and coincidence, so I often waited for the right chance to appear.
- **Turning point:** I watched a one-hour Bilibili interview with research scientist Shunyu Yao. His account of continuing to learn, applying to companies, and sending out his CV showed me how people can actively create opportunities.
- **New opinion:** If I want something, I should at least try. Action can create choices that would not exist if I only waited.
- **Concrete proof of the new mindset:** I changed the way I studied. Instead of waiting for teachers, classes, or ideal conditions to solve my learning problems, I started using AI proactively and practising speaking more actively.

### Possible opening

> I used to believe that good opportunities would eventually come if I waited long enough, but around the end of my third year of college, I started to see things differently.

---

## Cue card C - EV06: An important decision you made in life

### Best spotlight for this cue card

Focus on the decision to stop waiting for ideal conditions and take greater responsibility for my own learning and opportunities.

- **Earlier mindset:** Opportunities seemed to depend mainly on luck or coincidence.
- **Decision:** I decided to take initiative and create more options through action.
- **Concrete action:** I changed the way I prepared for IELTS and began using AI actively for speaking practice, explanation, correction, and planning.
- **Importance:** The decision affected both my study behaviour and the way I think about future opportunities.

A concise opening:

> One important decision I made was to stop waiting for ideal opportunities and start taking more initiative in my own learning and future plans.

---

## Cue card D - EV08: A plan that you had to change recently

### Best spotlight for this cue card

Focus on a recent adjustment to my IELTS preparation plan.

- **Original plan:** Complete a fixed set of listening, reading, writing, and speaking tasks each day.
- **Problem:** In practice, one day's assigned workload took about two days to finish.
- **Reason for change:** The fixed daily schedule was creating backlog and did not reflect my actual working pace.
- **New plan:** Use a flexible priority system in which tasks can move forward or roll over according to progress.
- **Priority rule:** Speaking receives high priority because its preparation timeline is especially urgent, while the other skills remain in the plan.
- **Current status:** The revised plan is still being used, so do not claim a final score improvement yet.

A concise version:

> I originally followed a fixed daily IELTS schedule, but I found that one day's workload was taking me almost two days. I therefore changed it into a flexible, priority-based plan that allows unfinished tasks to roll over.

## 1. Core story timeline

### Stage 1 — Before

- I was relatively passive about both opportunities and learning.
- I thought luck and coincidence were very important.
- In study, I relied heavily on memorizing vocabulary, lectures, online courses, and explanations from teachers.
- I sometimes knew a word when reading it but could not retrieve it quickly while speaking.
- Because I can be shy about asking questions, I did not always get the personal explanations I needed in a large class.

### Stage 2 — Turning point

- Near the end of my third year of college, I started thinking more seriously about my future.
- I noticed that classmates and other people often had more choices because they tried more things.
- In June, I watched a complete one-hour interview with Shunyu Yao on Bilibili.
- The program described his journey from studying physics as an undergraduate to becoming an AI research scientist and included his views on AI's current and future development.
- He also talked about continuing to learn, applying to companies, and sending out his CV instead of waiting for the perfect opportunity.
- That example made the idea of **taking initiative** feel concrete rather than abstract.

### Stage 3 — New mindset

> I used to believe that opportunities mainly came from luck, but now I think action creates opportunities.

Related ideas:

- Trying does not guarantee success, but not trying removes the possibility completely.
- Waiting can feel safe, but it can also make me passive.
- I should create more choices for myself instead of expecting choices to appear automatically.

### Stage 4 — Concrete behavior change: AI-assisted study

This is the strongest proof that the opinion change affected your real behavior.

- I stopped treating study as only memorization and passive input.
- I began practising spoken English in live AI conversations.
- I ask for questions to be repeated, slowed down, or explained in simpler language.
- I ask for grammar and vocabulary corrections after I speak.
- I build a phrase bank and a reusable Part 2 core-story bank.
- I use AI to clarify university material when a lecture or textbook explanation is not enough.
- I use AI to reduce repetitive work and make study time more efficient.

### Stage 5 - Recent adjustment to the IELTS plan

- The first plan assigned a fixed package of tasks to each day.
- My real pace showed that the workload was too rigid: one planned day took about two actual days.
- I changed to a priority-based plan rather than pretending the original schedule was still realistic.
- Tasks may be completed early or rolled forward when necessary.
- Speaking receives urgent attention, but listening, reading, and writing are not removed.

# Core Story 002 — Building a Line-Following Car as a Team

## 1. Core idea

During the last term, I worked in a four-person university team to build a small DIY car that could follow a line, use radar/sensors to detect obstacles, and move automatically. The project did not fully succeed, but it gives me a strong real story about teamwork, engineering, technical problems, imagination, time pressure, and learning from failure.

---

## 2. Background

- The project was part of a university course in the last term.
- The goal was to build a small autonomous car from scratch rather than simply copy an existing finished design.
- The car needed to follow a line and detect obstacles.
- There were four members in the team.

---

## 3. Team roles

The work was divided among the four members:

- one member designed the **radar / sensor PCB**;
- one member designed the **car-control PCB**, including the PID-control work;
- one member was responsible for the **lab notebook and final report**;
- my main responsibility was to **design and 3D-print the car structure and assemble the components**.

One teammate also coordinated the implementation plan:

- he planned how the team would build the car;
- he set project deadlines;
- he assigned the four main tasks to the team members;
- he regularly organised the work rather than making only one temporary plan.

### Useful simple IELTS version

> We divided the work according to different technical tasks, and my main responsibility was designing the car body, 3D-printing it, and assembling the components.

### The interface-board solution

After the radar PCB and car-control PCB had both been completed, connecting the two circuits became a problem. The teammate who organised the project proposed adding a small **interface board** between them. The team used this extra board, and it successfully solved the connection problem.

A concise version:

> When we had difficulty connecting the radar board to the car-control board, one teammate suggested adding a small interface board between them. His solution worked and allowed us to connect the two circuits successfully.

### Encouraging a teammate to redesign the control PCB

The teammate responsible for the car-control PCB and PID work fell behind schedule after several soldering attempts failed.

- I inspected the design and noticed that the PCB layout was too compact.
- The tight layout made the board difficult to solder and was a major reason for the repeated failures.
- I suggested changing both the PCB layout and the circuit connections.
- He initially disagreed because he had already invested substantial effort in the original design and did not want to lose that work.
- I used the repeated failures and the visible design problem as evidence that continuing with the same layout was unlikely to work.
- He eventually accepted the suggestion and changed the design.
- With the revised design, he successfully completed the task.
- He felt relieved, and I also felt relieved because the project could continue. I was proud that my technical judgment had proved correct.

A concise version:

> One teammate was reluctant to redesign his PCB because he had already invested a lot of work in it. I showed him that the layout was too compact and was causing repeated soldering failures. He eventually accepted my suggestion, changed the layout and connections, and completed the task successfully.

---

## 4. Main difficulty

The team faced several connected problems.

### Circuit and PCB delay

- Designing and producing the circuit board took longer than expected.
- Because of that delay, there was very little time left for PID testing and tuning.

### Mechanical problem

- The physical structure of the car also caused problems.
- The distance between the bottom of the car and the floor was too small.
- At times, the car did not move as expected because parts of the structure were too close to the ground.

### Control problem

- The PID parameters could not simply stay fixed.
- The team needed more time to test and adjust them under different conditions.
- Because testing time was limited, the final performance was unstable.

---

## 5. Result

- The project did **not fully succeed** in the final test.
- The failure was not caused by one single mistake.
- It came from several factors: circuit delays, limited testing time, mechanical design problems, and insufficient PID tuning.

This is useful for IELTS because the story does not need a perfect ending. The important part is explaining what happened and what I learned.

---

## 6. What I learned

- A technical project needs enough time for **integration and testing**, not only individual component design.
- Mechanical design, electronics, and control algorithms affect one another.
- A team can divide tasks, but members still need to communicate because one person's delay can affect everyone else's work.
- A failed result can still be valuable if I understand the causes and know what I would improve next time.

### Possible reflection

> Looking back, I realize that building each part separately was not enough. We also needed much more time to test how all the parts worked together.

# Independent Card — OB01: Paid Use of Plastic Shopping Bags

## Why this is an independent card

This is a real national regulation selected for **OB01 — A law or regulation about environmental protection**. It is separate from the Ershou Lake story because the lake cleanup has no verified legal title.

The central chain is:

> national regulation → effective from 1 June 2008 → supermarkets, shopping malls, and open markets → plastic shopping bags must be sold rather than given away → clear price → encourage people to use fewer bags → conserve resources and protect the environment

---

## 1. Verified facts

- The official Chinese title is **《商品零售场所塑料购物袋有偿使用管理办法》**.
- It was issued jointly by the Ministry of Commerce, the National Development and Reform Commission, and the former State Administration for Industry and Commerce.
- It took effect on **1 June 2008**.
- It applies to retail venues including supermarkets, shopping malls, and open markets.
- These retailers must charge consumers for plastic shopping bags and clearly display the price.
- They may not provide the bags free of charge or disguise a free bag through another promotion.
- The stated purpose is to conserve resources, protect the environment, and encourage consumers to use fewer plastic shopping bags.
- A later national measure effective in 2023 still requires retail businesses to follow this paid-use regulation.

---

## 2. Speakable English blocks

### Introduce the regulation

> I'd like to talk about China's regulation on the paid use of plastic shopping bags.

### Explain the rule

> It took effect in 2008 and requires supermarkets, shopping malls, and open markets to charge customers for plastic shopping bags instead of giving them away for free.

### Explain the purpose

> Its main purpose is to reduce unnecessary use of plastic bags, conserve resources, and protect the environment.

### Explain why the design makes sense

> The rule does not completely ban plastic bags. Instead, it makes their cost visible, which can encourage shoppers to bring reusable bags or think twice before taking a new one.

---

- provide something free of charge
