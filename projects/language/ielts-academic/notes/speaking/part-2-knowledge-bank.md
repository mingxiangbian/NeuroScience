# IELTS Speaking Part 2 Knowledge Bank

**Purpose:** Collect flexible personal material before trying to produce a full two-minute answer.

This is **not** a memorised-answer bank. It is a reusable story bank. Each story should contain enough real details to fit several cue cards.

---

# How to build each knowledge card

For every Part 2 topic, collect:

1. **Story title**
2. **Core change, event, person, place, object, or experience**
3. **Background**
4. **Cause or motivation**
5. **Specific actions**
6. **One memorable scene**
7. **Results**
8. **Feelings and reflection**
9. **Useful vocabulary and phrases**
10. **Other cue cards that can reuse the story**
11. **One-minute preparation keywords**

Do not write a full script first. Build the raw material, organise it, and then practise speaking from keywords.

# Core Story 001 — From Waiting to Taking Initiative

## Why this is a core story

This is **one personal experience that can answer several different IELTS Part 2 cue cards**. Do not memorize two separate answers.

The central story is:

> I used to be more passive and wait for opportunities or help to come to me. Around the end of my third year of college, I started to believe that I should take more initiative. One concrete change was the way I studied: instead of relying mainly on memorization, classes, and teachers, I began using AI actively as a personal study assistant, especially for IELTS speaking practice.

The facts stay mostly the same. **Only the spotlight changes according to the cue card.**

---

## Cue card A — Describe a change that you made recently

You should say:

- what the change was
- what caused the change
- what you did for the change
- how you feel about the change

### Best spotlight for this cue card

Focus mainly on the **study-routine change**.

- **Before:** I studied more passively, especially through vocabulary memorization, lectures, online courses, and material prepared by other people.
- **Change:** I began using AI actively as a learning assistant and added much more speaking practice.
- **Cause:** I needed a more efficient and personalized way to prepare for IELTS, and I had started to believe that I should take more initiative instead of waiting for the perfect learning environment.
- **Action:** I began having live English conversations, asking for explanations and corrections, building phrase and Part 2 story banks, and using AI to clarify difficult academic ideas.
- **Result:** My learning became more active, personalized, and efficient.

### Possible opening

> One important change I made recently was changing the way I study. I used to rely much more on passive learning, but now I try to take more initiative and use AI actively as a study partner.

---

## Cue card B — Describe a time when you changed an important opinion of yours

You should say:

- when you changed your opinion
- what your original opinion was
- why you changed it
- how you felt about the experience

### Best spotlight for this cue card

Focus mainly on the **mindset change**.

- **When:** Around the end of my third year of college.
- **Old opinion:** I believed that opportunities depended heavily on luck and coincidence, so I often waited for the right chance to appear.
- **Turning point:** I saw people around me actively create opportunities. One memorable example was a social-media video about a person working in AI who kept learning, applying to companies, and sending out his CV instead of simply waiting.
- **New opinion:** If I want something, I should at least try. Action can create choices that would not exist if I only waited.
- **Concrete proof of the new mindset:** I changed the way I studied. Instead of waiting for teachers, classes, or ideal conditions to solve my learning problems, I started using AI proactively and practising speaking more actively.

### Possible opening

> I used to believe that good opportunities would eventually come if I waited long enough, but around the end of my third year of college, I started to see things differently.

---

## 1. Core story timeline

### Stage 1 — Before

- I was relatively passive about both opportunities and learning.
- I thought luck and coincidence were very important.
- In study, I relied heavily on memorizing vocabulary, lectures, online courses, and explanations from teachers.
- I sometimes knew a word when reading it but could not retrieve it quickly while speaking.
- Because I can be shy about asking questions, I did not always get the personal explanations I needed in a large class.

### Stage 2 — Turning point

- Near the end of my third year of college, I started thinking more seriously about my future.
- I noticed that classmates and other people often had more choices because they tried more things.
- I watched a social-media video about someone working in artificial intelligence.
- He talked about continuing to learn, applying to many companies, and sending his CV out instead of waiting for the perfect opportunity.
- That example made the idea of **taking initiative** feel concrete rather than abstract.

### Stage 3 — New mindset

> I used to believe that opportunities mainly came from luck, but now I think action creates opportunities.

Related ideas:

- Trying does not guarantee success, but not trying removes the possibility completely.
- Waiting can feel safe, but it can also make me passive.
- I should create more choices for myself instead of expecting choices to appear automatically.

### Stage 4 — Concrete behavior change: AI-assisted study

This is the strongest proof that the opinion change affected your real behavior.

- I stopped treating study as only memorization and passive input.
- I began practising spoken English in live AI conversations.
- I ask for questions to be repeated, slowed down, or explained in simpler language.
- I ask for grammar and vocabulary corrections after I speak.
- I build a phrase bank and a reusable Part 2 core-story bank.
- I use AI to clarify university material when a lecture or textbook explanation is not enough.
- I use AI to reduce repetitive work and make study time more efficient.

---

## 2. Memorable scenes

### Scene A — Social-media turning point

> I remember watching a video in which someone working in AI explained that he kept sending his CV to different companies and continued trying even when opportunities did not appear immediately.

Use this scene when the question is about:

- changing an opinion
- inspiration
- social media
- a person who influenced you
- persistence

### Scene B — AI speaking practice

> During an IELTS speaking practice session, I did not understand a cue-card word, so I asked the AI to slow down and explain it. After I understood the meaning, I was able to continue the answer instead of simply giving up.

Use this scene when the question is about:

- a recent change
- technology
- learning
- solving a problem
- communication
- becoming more independent

---

## 3. Results

### Practical results

- I can practise speaking more frequently.
- I receive feedback immediately.
- I have a more personalized study process.
- I can identify weak points instead of only repeating familiar material.
- I am training vocabulary retrieval, not only vocabulary recognition.
- I use study time more efficiently.

### Mindset results

- I am becoming more willing to take initiative.
- I am less likely to wait for the perfect condition before starting.
- I see action as a way to create possibilities.
- I feel more responsible for my own progress.

---

## 4. Feelings and reflection

Some feelings have not been fully confirmed yet, so keep this section flexible and only use wording that feels true in the exam.

Possible ideas:

- more motivated
- more independent
- more confident
- slightly nervous because taking action also means risking failure
- relieved because I no longer have to wait for perfect conditions
- more responsible for my own future

### Strong reflection

> Looking back, I realize that the biggest change was not simply using a new technology. It was becoming more proactive and taking greater responsibility for my own learning and opportunities.

# Core Story 002 — Building a Line-Following Car as a Team

## 1. Core idea

During the last term, I worked in a four-person university team to build a small DIY car that could follow a line, use radar/sensors to detect obstacles, and move automatically. The project did not fully succeed, but it gives me a strong real story about teamwork, engineering, technical problems, imagination, time pressure, and learning from failure.

---

## 2. Background

- The project was part of a university course in the last term.
- The goal was to build a small autonomous car from scratch rather than simply copy an existing finished design.
- The car needed to follow a line and detect obstacles.
- There were four members in the team.

---

## 3. Team roles

The work was divided among the four members:

- one member focused mainly on **PID control**;
- one member designed the **driving circuit / PCB**;
- one member was responsible for the **lab notebook and final report**;
- my main responsibility was to **design and 3D-print the car structure and assemble the components**.

### Useful simple IELTS version

> We divided the work according to different technical tasks, and my main responsibility was designing the car body, 3D-printing it, and assembling the components.

---

## 4. Main difficulty

The team faced several connected problems.

### Circuit and PCB delay

- Designing and producing the circuit board took longer than expected.
- Because of that delay, there was very little time left for PID testing and tuning.

### Mechanical problem

- The physical structure of the car also caused problems.
- The distance between the bottom of the car and the floor was too small.
- At times, the car did not move as expected because parts of the structure were too close to the ground.

### Control problem

- The PID parameters could not simply stay fixed.
- The team needed more time to test and adjust them under different conditions.
- Because testing time was limited, the final performance was unstable.

---

## 5. Result

- The project did **not fully succeed** in the final test.
- The failure was not caused by one single mistake.
- It came from several factors: circuit delays, limited testing time, mechanical design problems, and insufficient PID tuning.

This is useful for IELTS because the story does not need a perfect ending. The important part is explaining what happened and what I learned.

---

## 6. What I learned

- A technical project needs enough time for **integration and testing**, not only individual component design.
- Mechanical design, electronics, and control algorithms affect one another.
- A team can divide tasks, but members still need to communicate because one person's delay can affect everyone else's work.
- A failed result can still be valuable if I understand the causes and know what I would improve next time.

### Possible reflection

> Looking back, I realize that building each part separately was not enough. We also needed much more time to test how all the parts worked together.

---

## 7. Cue cards this story can cover

### Strong / already supported by the real experience

#### EV09 — A time when you worked in a group

Focus on:

> four members → different responsibilities → your 3D-printing and assembly role → technical problems → final result → teamwork lesson

#### EV05 — A challenging technological problem you faced

Focus on:

> DIY autonomous car → circuit delay → little testing time → mechanical/PID problems → attempted adjustments → what you learned

#### EV14 — A time when you used imagination

Focus on:

> no ready-made final design → designing the car structure → deciding how to arrange and assemble components → 3D printing → testing the physical design

### Potentially reusable, but more real details are needed first

#### PE06 — A person who gave a clever solution to a problem

This can use the same project **only if** one teammate genuinely proposed a specific clever solution that you remember.

#### EV13 — A time when you encouraged someone to do something they didn't want to do

Use this project **only if** you genuinely encouraged a teammate to take an action they were reluctant to take.

#### PE10 — A person who makes plans a lot

Use this project **only if** one real teammate was clearly the person who organised schedules, divided tasks, or made detailed plans.

### Additional confirmed use

#### EV03 — A time when you received a message or email and it took you a long time to reply

This project can support EV03 naturally. Around six days before the deadline, the team was under heavy time pressure and spent most of the day working on the car. Because I was so focused on the project, I barely checked my phone. Messages from my parents or friends sometimes stayed unread for much longer than usual, so I replied several hours later.

Natural version:

> We were only about six days away from the deadline, so I was extremely busy with the project and barely had time to check my phone. As a result, I sometimes didn't see messages from my parents or friends until much later, and I replied several hours later than I normally would.

### Conditional use

#### EV19 — An occasion when you were not allowed to use a mobile phone

Use this project for EV19 **only if there was a genuine rule or agreement restricting phone use** during the work session, for example a lab rule or a real team agreement to put phones away. Simply being too busy to check the phone is **not the same as being not allowed to use it**.

Do not invent these details just to increase coverage.

# Core Story 003 — Reconnecting with Friends at a Spider-Man Movie

## 1. Core idea

After not seeing some friends for a long time, we arranged a movie outing because we are all Marvel fans. We chose a Spider-Man film, I booked the tickets, and the outing gave us a natural chance to reconnect and enjoy being together again.

---

## 2. Background

- We had not met for quite a long time.
- We are all interested in Marvel movies and comics.
- When a Spider-Man film came out, it gave us an easy reason to meet again.
- Because we already knew the characters and the Marvel universe well, we had plenty to talk about before and after the movie.

---

## 3. My role

- I helped turn the idea into an actual plan.
- I booked the movie tickets.
- The plan itself was simple, which made it easy for everyone to join.

A natural sentence:

> Since we hadn't seen each other in ages, I suggested that we watch the new Spider-Man movie together and I booked the tickets.

---

## 4. Vivid details

The strongest details are not complicated facts about the movie. They are the small interactions between friends.

- We talked about the characters because all of us were Marvel fans.
- After the movie, we discussed what we liked and how we would rate it.
- One friend thought it was the best of the three Spider-Man films starring Tom Holland.
- We also talked about future Marvel films we were looking forward to.
- The playful discussion made the outing feel more personal than simply sitting in a cinema.

A useful line:

> We ended up talking about the movie for quite a while, and one of my friends even said it was his favourite of the three Tom Holland Spider-Man films.

---

## 5. Why I enjoyed the experience

Do not stop at:

> It improved our friendship.

Extend the idea in three layers:

### Reconnection

> We hadn't met for a long time, so the outing gave us a chance to reconnect.

### Feeling

> I felt relaxed and comfortable because I was spending time with people who shared the same interests.

### Small moment

> Our playful debate about the movie reminded me how much I had missed talking with them.

A natural combined version:

> What made the experience special was that we hadn't seen each other in ages. Watching the movie gave us a reason to meet again, but the part I enjoyed most was actually the conversation afterward. We joked about the characters, compared our opinions, and I realized how much I had missed spending time with them.

---

## 6. Result and reflection

- The outing helped us reconnect after a long period without meeting.
- It reminded me that friendships need some active effort to maintain.
- A simple shared interest can be enough to bring people together again.
- The experience was memorable mainly because of the people, not because the activity itself was unusual.

Possible reflection:

> Looking back, I realize that the movie was really just an excuse to meet. What mattered most was reconnecting with my friends.

---

- debate something playfully

## 8. Confirmed and conditional cue-card coverage

### Strongest confirmed uses

This story is naturally useful for:

- an enjoyable experience with friends
- a time you reconnected with someone

- an activity you enjoyed because of the people you were with

### Conditional use in the current May–August topic bank

- **EV01 — A happy event you organized:** usable if you present the movie outing as the event you arranged and clearly explain your role in booking/planning it.
- **OB11 — A movie you watched recently:** usable only if the Spider-Man movie was genuinely recent enough for you to describe honestly.

### Gathering boundary

The earlier conversation used this outing as the real example for a **small gathering with friends**. That is valid when the prompt only requires people to meet and enjoy an activity together. In that version, focus on:

> friends had not met for a long time → shared Marvel interest → arranged the outing → watched the movie → talked afterward → reconnected

The word **gathering** does not automatically mean a birthday party. The same story stops being suitable when the cue card specifically requires details that did not happen.

### Do not force this story into

- EV10 — a special cake
- OB07 — food eaten during a special event
- EV12 — an occasion when many people were smiling, unless you have a real scene involving a larger group
- birthday or party questions that specifically require decorations, gifts, a cake, special food, or other details this outing did not actually contain

This story therefore covers a **general small gathering**, but not every birthday or party topic. Keep it because it is authentic and easy to remember, while preserving the factual boundary above.

# Core Story 004 — Ershou Lake: From a Polluted Lake to a Cleaner Public Place

## Why this is now a core story

This story is stronger than the earlier backup version because it now has a clear **before-and-after change**, a real place, a family connection, an environmental-policy angle, and a future-policy opinion.

The central chain is:

> Ershou Lake near my hometown → dirty and unpleasant in the past → local government introduced environmental measures and developed the area → the lake became cleaner and more attractive → more people started visiting → I now walk there with my family → I would support stricter anti-littering and ecosystem-protection rules

Do **not** invent the official name of an existing law. At the moment, the confirmed fact is that local environmental measures or cleanup actions were introduced; the exact legal title has not been verified.

---

## 1. Background

- The lake is called **Ershou Lake** and is near my hometown.
- My grandparents used to go fishing there in the past.
- It was not a particularly famous place before.
- In the past, the lake was dirty and sometimes smelled unpleasant.
- Later, the local government introduced environmental cleanup or protection measures and developed the surrounding area for tourism.
- The area gradually became cleaner and more attractive to visitors.
- I now sometimes walk around the lake with my family.

---

## 2. The environmental change

### Before

- The lake looked less attractive.
- The water and surrounding environment were dirtier.
- There was an unpleasant smell.
- The area was not a major place for visitors.

### After

- Environmental cleanup and management improved the area.
- The lake became cleaner.
- Tourism and nearby commercial activity developed.
- More people began visiting the area.
- It became a more pleasant place for local families to walk and relax.

A natural sentence:

> One thing I've noticed is that the whole area has changed a lot. It used to be quite dirty and unpleasant, but after the local government introduced environmental measures, it gradually became much cleaner and more attractive.

---

## 3. Personal connection

This should not become only a general environmental-policy answer. Keep the story personal.

- My grandparents used to fish there.
- I now walk around the lake with my family.
- Walking there helps me relax and clear my mind.
- It gives me a chance to take my mind off study pressure.
- The change is easy for me to notice because I have a family connection with the place.

Possible line:

> What makes the change meaningful to me is that the lake is close to my hometown and my grandparents used to fish there, so it is not just a tourist attraction to me.

---

## 4. Cue Card A — OB01: A law or regulation about environmental protection

### Safe angle

Because the exact official name of the law or regulation has **not** been confirmed, do not invent one.

The safest preparation is:

> local environmental regulations or government measures aimed at cleaning the lake and controlling pollution

Focus on:

> old condition → environmental measures → cleaner lake → tourism/public benefit → why you support the policy

### Who benefits

- local residents
- families who use the area for walking and recreation
- visitors and tourists
- local businesses that depend on a cleaner environment
- fish and other parts of the lake ecosystem

### How I feel

- supportive
- pleased that the environment improved
- impressed by the visible change
- aware that enforcement still matters

Possible reflection:

> Looking back, I realize that environmental rules can feel abstract until you see a place change in front of you. In this case, the improvement is very visible.

---

## 5. Cue Card B — OB09: A new law you would like to introduce in your country

This is a **future proposal**, so keep it clearly separate from the existing cleanup measures.

### My proposed policy

I would like stricter rules around lakes and public waterways, especially:

- fines for people who throw rubbish into the lake or leave litter nearby
- stronger protection for the local ecosystem
- limits or controls on fishing practices if they damage fish populations or the balance of the ecosystem

A natural version:

> If I could introduce a new rule, I would make littering around lakes much more strictly controlled, with clear fines for people who throw rubbish into the water. I would also like stronger rules to protect fish and the wider ecosystem.

### Why I would introduce it

- Cleaning a lake once is not enough.
- The improved environment needs to be protected over time.
- More visitors can also create more rubbish and pressure on the ecosystem.
- Environmental protection works better when people know there are clear consequences for harmful behavior.

---
