# IELTS Speaking Phrase Bank

**Goal:** Turn familiar phrases into phrases you can retrieve naturally during the IELTS Speaking test.

**Current target:** IELTS Speaking Band 7.0  
**Method:** Learn the meaning, use a short pattern, extend it when needed, and reuse it in different topics.

---

## How to use this file

For each phrase:

1. Read the meaning.
2. Say the short example aloud.
3. Make one sentence about your own life.
4. Use the phrase again in a different IELTS question.
5. Mark it **Automatic** only after you can use it naturally without being reminded.

Suggested status labels:

- **New** — I understand it, but cannot use it yet.
- **Active practice** — I can use it with some thinking time.
- **Automatic** — I can use it naturally while speaking.

---

## 1. Personally, I think...

**Function:** Give your opinion directly.  
**Status:** Active practice

**Pattern**

> Personally, I think + opinion.

**Short example**

> Personally, I think learning English is a good way to understand myself better.

**Longer example**

> Personally, I think learning English is valuable because it allows me to express myself in another language and communicate with people from different backgrounds.

**Common mistake**

Do not add too many ideas before clearly stating your opinion.

---

## 2. The main reason is that...

**Function:** Introduce your strongest reason.  
**Status:** Active practice

**Pattern**

> The main reason is that + complete clause.

**Short example**

> The main reason is that English gives me more ways to express myself.

**Longer example**

> The main reason is that learning English gives me access to more information, especially courses, articles, and conversations that may not be available in my first language.

**Common mistake**

Say:

> The main reason is that...

Not:

> For the reasons...

---

## 3. For example,...

**Function:** Add a specific example.  
**Status:** Active practice

**Pattern**

> For example, + specific situation.

**Short example**

> For example, I use English when I talk with an AI assistant.

**Longer example**

> For example, when I practise IELTS speaking with an AI assistant, I can repeat a question, ask for simpler explanations, and receive corrections immediately.

**Tip**

One clear example is usually enough in IELTS Speaking Part 1.

---

## 4. One thing I've noticed is that...

**Function:** Introduce a personal observation or pattern.  
**Status:** Active practice

**Pattern**

> One thing I've noticed is that + complete clause.

**Short example**

> One thing I've noticed is that I remember new words better when I use them in conversation.

**Your corrected example**

> One thing I've noticed is that communicating with others helps me improve my English.

**Longer example**

> One thing I've noticed is that when I use a new word in a real conversation, I understand it more deeply and remember it for much longer.

**Common mistakes**

Say:

> One thing I've noticed is that...

Not:

> One thing I've noticed that...

Also say:

> Communicating **with** others **helps** me...

---

## 5. It depends on the situation.

**Function:** Give a balanced answer when the answer is not simply yes or no.  
**Status:** Active practice

**Pattern**

> It depends on the situation. Sometimes..., but...

**Short example**

> It depends on the situation. Sometimes I enjoy learning English, but memorising long word lists can be boring.

**Longer example**

> It depends on the situation. I enjoy learning English when I can communicate with other people and feel that I am improving, but I lose motivation when the practice is repetitive.

**Common mistake**

Say:

> It depends **on** the situation.

Not:

> It depends the situation.

---

## 6. Over time, I've become more...

**Function:** Describe a gradual change from the past until now.  
**Status:** Active practice

**Pattern**

> Over time, I've become more + adjective.

**Short example**

> Over time, I've become more confident speaking English.

**Other useful adjectives**

- confident
- patient
- independent
- organised
- comfortable
- open-minded

**Longer example**

> Over time, I've become more confident speaking English because I now practise regularly and worry less about making small mistakes.

**Common mistakes**

Say:

> I've become more **confident**.

Not:

> I've become more **confidence**.

“Confident” is an adjective. “Confidence” is a noun.

---

## 7. What I like most is that...

**Function:** Highlight the best or most important feature.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Pattern**

> What I like most is that + complete clause.

**Short example**

> What I like most is that walking after dinner helps me relax.

**Longer example**

> What I like most is that walking after dinner helps me relax after a busy day because I spend most of my time studying at a computer, so the walk gives me fresh air and time to organise my thoughts.

**Extension shape**

> Main idea + because + detail + result.

---

## 8. Another thing worth mentioning is that...

**Function:** Add another relevant point naturally.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Pattern**

> Another thing worth mentioning is that + complete clause.

**Short example**

> Another thing worth mentioning is that walking helps me sleep better.

**Longer example**

> Another thing worth mentioning is that this habit gives me time to review what happened during the day, which helps me clear my mind before I go to bed.

**Common mistake**

Say:

> Another thing **worth mentioning is that**...

Not:

> Another thing **we're mentioning**...

---

## 9. Looking back, I realize that...

**Function:** Add reflection, a lesson, or a change in perspective.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Pattern**

> Looking back, I realize that + lesson or reflection.

**Short example**

> Looking back, I realize that regular practice is more useful than memorising long lists.

**Longer example**

> Looking back, I realize that I improved more when I started using English actively instead of only reading vocabulary and trying to remember definitions.

**Best use**

This phrase is especially useful near the end of an IELTS Speaking Part 2 answer.

## 10. I used to believe that...

**Function:** Introduce an opinion or belief you had in the past but no longer fully agree with.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Pattern**

> I used to believe that + old opinion.

**Short example**

> I used to believe that good opportunities would simply appear if I waited long enough.

**Longer example**

> I used to believe that luck and coincidence played the biggest role in finding opportunities, so I often waited instead of actively looking for them.

**Useful contrast**

> I used to believe that..., but now I think...

---

## 11. What changed my mind was...

**Function:** Explain the event, person, experience, or evidence that caused you to change an opinion.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Pattern**

> What changed my mind was + noun / noun phrase.

or

> What changed my mind was seeing / hearing / experiencing + detail.

**Short example**

> What changed my mind was seeing other people actively create opportunities for themselves.

**Longer example**

> What changed my mind was watching a video about someone working in artificial intelligence who kept applying to companies instead of waiting for the perfect opportunity to come to him.

**Tip**

This phrase is especially useful for IELTS Speaking Part 2 topics about a changed opinion, advice, inspiration, or a turning point.

## 12. That experience made me realize that...
13. One concrete change I made was...
14. Instead of waiting for..., I started...
15. This was part of a bigger change in my mindset.
16. We divided the work according to...
17. My main responsibility was...
18. We ran into a problem when...

**Function:** State the lesson or new opinion you developed after an experience.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Pattern**

> That experience made me realize that + new understanding.

**Short example**

> That experience made me realize that opportunities usually require action.

**Longer example**

> That experience made me realize that if I want something, I should actively look for opportunities instead of simply waiting for them to appear.

**Useful alternative**

> It made me realize that...

**Common grammar point**

Say:

> If I **don't try**, the opportunity may never appear.

Not:

> If I **not try**...


## 13. One concrete change I made was...

**Function:** Connect an abstract opinion or lesson to a real action.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Pattern**

> One concrete change I made was + noun / -ing form.

**Short example**

> One concrete change I made was practising English more actively.

**Longer example**

> One concrete change I made was using AI as a study partner instead of relying only on vocabulary lists and classroom explanations.

**Best use**

Use this when you need to prove that a change in mindset affected your real behavior.

---

## 14. Instead of waiting for..., I started...

**Function:** Show a clear contrast between old and new behavior.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Pattern**

> Instead of waiting for + noun/person/event, I started + -ing form.

**Short example**

> Instead of waiting for the perfect opportunity, I started looking for one myself.

**Longer example**

> Instead of waiting for teachers or classes to solve every problem, I started using AI actively to ask questions, practise speaking, and get immediate feedback.

**Common grammar point**

After **started**, use either:

> started using...

or

> started to use...

Both are natural.

---

## 15. This was part of a bigger change in my mindset.
16. We divided the work according to...
17. My main responsibility was...
18. We ran into a problem when...

**Function:** Link one small behavior change to a broader personal change.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Pattern**

> This was part of a bigger change in my mindset: + explanation.

**Short example**

> This was part of a bigger change in my mindset: I wanted to become more proactive.

**Longer example**

> This was part of a bigger change in my mindset: I no longer wanted to wait for opportunities or ideal conditions, and I wanted to take more responsibility for my own progress.

**Best use**

Especially useful when one core story needs to answer both a **behavior-change** cue card and an **opinion-change** cue card.

---

## 16. We divided the work according to...

**Function:** Describe teamwork and role allocation clearly.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Pattern**

> We divided the work according to + roles / tasks / strengths.

**Short example**

> We divided the work according to different technical tasks.

**Longer example**

> We divided the work according to different technical tasks, so one person handled PID control, another designed the circuit, and I focused on the 3D-printed structure and assembly.

---

## 17. My main responsibility was...

**Function:** Explain your personal role inside a group project.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Pattern**

> My main responsibility was + noun / -ing form.

**Short example**

> My main responsibility was designing and assembling the car body.

**Longer example**

> My main responsibility was designing the physical structure, 3D-printing it, and assembling the electronic components onto the car.

**Grammar note**

After **was**, use a noun phrase or an **-ing** form naturally:

> My responsibility was **designing** the structure.

---

## 18. We ran into a problem when...

**Function:** Introduce a difficulty naturally in a Part 2 story.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Pattern**

> We ran into a problem when + event / situation.

**Short example**

> We ran into a problem when the circuit took longer than expected.

**Longer example**

> We ran into a problem when the circuit and PCB took longer than expected, which left us with very little time to test and tune the PID controller.

**Useful alternative**

> We ran into several problems during the final stage of the project.

---

## 19. We hadn't seen each other in ages.

**Function:** Explain that you are reconnecting with someone after a long time.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Short example**

> We hadn't seen each other in ages, so we decided to watch a movie together.

**Useful alternative**

> We hadn't met for quite a long time.

**Best use**

Friendship, reunion, outings, and memorable-event stories.

---

## 20. What made the experience special was that...

**Function:** Move from describing an event to explaining why it mattered to you.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Pattern**

> What made the experience special was that + personal reason.

**Short example**

> What made the experience special was that it gave us a chance to reconnect.

**Longer example**

> What made the experience special was that we hadn't seen each other for a long time, so the movie gave us a simple reason to meet and catch up again.

---

## 21. We ended up talking about...

**Function:** Add a natural follow-up detail after an activity.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Pattern**

> We ended up talking about + noun / topic.

**Short example**

> We ended up talking about our favourite Marvel characters.

**Longer example**

> We ended up talking about the movie for quite a while, comparing our opinions and discussing which Spider-Man film we liked most.

**Why it helps**

This phrase is useful for extending a Part 2 story without forcing another separate reason.

---

## 22. It helps me clear my mind.

**Function:** Explain that an activity helps you feel mentally calmer or think more clearly.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Short example**

> Walking around the lake helps me clear my mind.

**Longer example**

> When I feel stressed from studying, walking around the lake with my family helps me clear my mind and feel more relaxed.

**Common mistake**

Say:

> It helps me **clear my mind**.

Not:

> It gives me a relax.

You can say **It helps me relax** or **It makes me feel relaxed**.

---

## 23. It gives me a chance to take my mind off...

**Function:** Explain that something temporarily distracts you from stress or a problem.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Pattern**

> It gives me a chance to take my mind off + noun.

**Short example**

> It gives me a chance to take my mind off my studies for a while.

**Longer example**

> A quiet walk with my family gives me a chance to take my mind off academic pressure for a while and come back feeling fresher.

**Best use**

Useful for places, hobbies, healthy habits, free-time activities, and relaxing experiences.

---


## 24. I was so busy with... that I barely had time to...

**Function:** Explain strong time pressure and why something was delayed.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Pattern**

> I was so busy with + noun that I barely had time to + verb.

**Short example**

> I was so busy with the project that I barely had time to check my phone.

**Longer example**

> We were only a few days away from the deadline, and I was so busy with the project that I barely had time to check my phone or reply to messages.

**Useful note**

**Barely** means *almost not*. It is very useful when explaining limited time.

---

## 25. I didn't see the message until much later.

**Function:** Explain naturally why a reply was delayed.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Short example**

> I didn't see the message until much later, so I replied several hours late.

**Longer example**

> Because I was concentrating on the project, I didn't see my friend's message until much later, and I replied several hours later than I normally would.

**Best use**

Useful for message, email, phone-use, deadline, and busy-schedule stories.

---


## 26. The local government introduced environmental measures to...

**Function:** Describe a government action without inventing the exact official name of a law.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Pattern**

> The local government introduced environmental measures to + verb.

**Short example**

> The local government introduced environmental measures to clean up the lake.

**Longer example**

> The local government introduced environmental measures to reduce pollution and make the area cleaner and more attractive to local residents and visitors.

**Best use**

Use this when you know what the government did but do not know the exact official title of the policy.

---

## 27. If I could introduce a new rule, I would...

**Function:** Introduce a future law, regulation, or policy proposal clearly.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Pattern**

> If I could introduce a new rule, I would + base verb.

**Short example**

> If I could introduce a new rule, I would fine people who throw rubbish into the lake.

**Longer example**

> If I could introduce a new rule, I would make littering around lakes much more strictly controlled and introduce clear penalties for people who damage the environment.

---

## 28. Cleaning the area once is not enough.

**Function:** Explain why long-term protection and enforcement are necessary.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Short example**

> Cleaning the area once is not enough; the improvement also needs to be maintained.

**Longer example**

> Cleaning the area once is not enough, because if there are no clear rules or regular protection measures, the same pollution problems may gradually return.

**Best use**

Environmental protection, public places, government policy, and long-term change.

---


## 29. We looked at several options before making a decision.

**Function:** Show a careful decision-making process.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Short example**

> We looked at several options before making a decision.

**Longer example**

> We looked at several universities before making a decision because we wanted to compare both the available majors and the entry requirements.

---

## 30. We tried to balance... with...

**Function:** Explain that two different factors were both important.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Pattern**

> We tried to balance + factor A + with + factor B.

**Short example**

> We tried to balance what was realistic with what she genuinely wanted.

**Longer example**

> We tried to balance the universities that were realistic for her grades with the subjects she was genuinely interested in studying.

---

## 31. We worked through the decision together.

**Function:** Describe collaboration on a difficult choice.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Short example**

> We worked through the decision together.

**Longer example**

> We worked through the decision together by researching the options, comparing their advantages and disadvantages, and listening to what my sister actually wanted.

**Best use**

Family decisions, teamwork, giving advice, and solving a problem together.

---


## 32. Every visit feels almost the same.

**Function:** Explain why a place or activity feels repetitive.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Short example**

> Every visit feels almost the same.

**Longer example**

> Every visit feels almost the same because I usually see the same shops, the same promotions, and the same kinds of activities.

---

## 33. There is nothing particularly unique about it.

**Function:** Explain that a place is not bad, but does not stand out.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Short example**

> There is nothing particularly unique about it.

**Longer example**

> There is nothing particularly unique about the shopping center because most of the things I can do there are available in many other malls.

---

## 34. I usually go there only when I have a specific reason.

**Function:** Explain that you do not visit a place for enjoyment itself.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Short example**

> I usually go there only when I have a specific reason.

**Longer example**

> I usually go there only when I have a specific reason, such as watching a movie with friends or having coffee with my parents.

---

## 35. It is convenient, but I wouldn't call it exciting.

**Function:** Give a balanced opinion with one positive and one negative point.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Short example**

> It is convenient, but I wouldn't call it exciting.

**Best use**

Places, shopping centers, neighborhoods, apps, and everyday services.

---


## 36. I ended up spending more than I expected.

**Function:** Explain that the final cost was higher than your original plan.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Short example**

> I ended up spending more than I expected.

**Longer example**

> I ended up spending more than I expected because I chose a model with better performance and more storage.

---

## 37. I rely on it for a lot of everyday tasks.

**Function:** Explain why an object or device is important in daily life.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Short example**

> I rely on it for a lot of everyday tasks.

**Longer example**

> I rely on my smartphone for a lot of everyday tasks, including communication, navigation, studying, and digital payments.

---

## 38. Considering how often I use it, I think it was worth the money.

**Function:** Give a balanced conclusion about an expensive purchase.  
**Status:** New / active practice

**Short example**

> Considering how often I use it, I think it was worth the money.

**Best use**

Expensive items, technology, useful possessions, and purchase decisions.

# Useful closing phrases

## So overall,...

> So overall, learning English helps me grow and communicate more confidently.

## So that's why...

> So that's why I try to practise speaking every day.

## That's basically how I feel.

Use this as a safe ending when your answer is already complete.

---

# Sentence-length strategy

Use a mixture of sentence lengths:

- **Short sentence:** state the main idea clearly.
- **Medium or long sentence:** explain the reason or give detail.
- **Short sentence:** conclude or move to the next point.

A useful rhythm is:

> Short → longer explanation → short conclusion.

For your current level, clarity is more important than forcing complicated sentences.

---

# Daily retrieval practice

## Step 1 — Review

Choose three older phrases and say one sentence with each.

## Step 2 — New phrases

Learn up to three new phrases, but do not keep adding phrases that you already use naturally.

## Step 3 — Forced use

Answer two IELTS questions and deliberately use the target phrases.

## Step 4 — Retrieval repair

When a word does not appear in your mind:

1. Explain it using simpler words.
2. Finish your answer without freezing.
3. Write down the missing word afterward.
4. Reuse it in two new spoken sentences.
